I also tried something myself to punish zigzagging and stopping.

Woerner, Benjamin, Novitzky & Leonard (MIT, Autonomous Robots 2019) — use  four axes you mention: safety, protocol compliance, spatial efficiency, temporal efficiency, validated with 6,150+ real vessel encounters on the water. I do not have access to their exact formulas, so I used their structure as a proven starting point and developed it myself — plus your two extra axes (number of maneuvers, smoothness).

The core of the design — and why "standing still" actually scores poorly: two hard gates, not one big weighted sum. With a naive weighted sum, "standing still" scored 0.70 — much too high, exactly your concern. The fix: (1) a collision = always 0.0, regardless of everything else; (2) failure to arrive = a maximum of 0.2, regardless of how "neat" the partial trajectory looked. Only trips that are both safe and arrive are assessed on the full weighted mix. I actually tested this, not just wrote it — five synthetic trajectories specifically designed to break the function (standstill, smooth-efficient, jerky-zigzag, real collision, unnecessary large detour), and that found two real bugs:

The "standstill wins falsely" error mentioned above.

The maneuver counter completely missed a continuous zigzag (counted it as 1 maneuver instead of dozens) — a continuous series of small opposing corrections never returns to "smooth," so the original logic saw it as one continuous maneuver. Fixed by also counting a direction reversal within a maneuver as a separate event. Final result, correctly ordered:

Scenario Score
Collision 0.000
Standing still / never arriving 0.200
Large unnecessary detour 0.791
Jerky zigzag (but fast/direct) 0.799
Smooth, purposeful, one clean maneuver 0.947
