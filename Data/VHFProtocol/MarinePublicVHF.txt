Channel 16 Calling Procedures
Channel 16 serves as the international calling and distress frequency, requiring specific procedures for initiating contact while maintaining availability for emergency communications.

Proper Channel 16 usage involves brief initial contact followed by immediate transfer to working channels, ensuring the international emergency frequency remains clear for distress and safety communications.

Receiving calls on Channel 16:
• Monitor Indication - RX symbol displays when receiving signals on current channel
• Handset Activation - Lift handset or activate alternative speaker device
• PTT Operation - Press Push To Talk button for transmission mode
• Station Acknowledgment - Repeat calling station name and identify your vessel
• Working Channel Suggestion - Propose appropriate channel for continued communication
• Transfer Confirmation - Await caller acknowledgment before changing channels
• Channel Selection - Switch to agreed frequency for message traffic

Making calls on Channel 16:
► Channel Selection - Ensure radio tuned to Channel 16 international frequency
► Listen Before Transmitting - Verify channel clear before initiating calls
► Station Identification - Call desired station three times with clear pronunciation
► Own Identification - State your vessel name clearly after "This is"
► Transmission Ending - Conclude with "Over" before releasing PTT button
► Response Monitoring - Listen for acknowledgment with RX mode active
► Working Channel Agreement - Propose specific channel for communication continuation
► Channel Transfer - Switch to agreed frequency after mutual confirmation

❔ Did you know? Single short press on 16 key provides immediate access to Channel 16 from any radio operational state.

POWER MANAGEMENT AND TRANSMISSION
VHF radio power management encompasses transmission power control, antenna systems, and operational modes designed to optimize communication range while conserving battery power and complying with regulatory transmission restrictions.

Transmission Power Control
Professional power management balances communication effectiveness with power conservation through selectable transmission power levels and automated power saving features integrated throughout the system operation.

Power level management:
1. High Power Mode - 12 or 25 watts standard transmission for maximum range coverage
2. Low Power Mode - 1 or 2 watt reduced transmission for close-range communication or at oil terminals
3. Power Toggle - 1W (or 2W) key switches between high and low power settings
4. LO Display Indicator - Visual confirmation of low power transmission mode
5. Automatic Restrictions - Certain channels limited to low power by regulation
6. Battery Conservation - Low power extends operational time during emergencies

Local Mode and Attenuation
Local mode provides specialized operational capability through 10 dB signal attenuation designed for close-proximity communication while reducing interference in congested areas.

This attenuation feature proves particularly valuable in port areas or when operating near other vessels where reduced signal strength prevents interference while maintaining adequate communication capability.

Local mode characteristics:
• Signal Attenuation - 10 dB reduction in both transmission and reception sensitivity
• Activation Method - specific key engages attenuation mode
• Interference Reduction - Minimizes signal overlap in congested communication areas
• Range Limitation - Deliberately reduced coverage for close-range applications

Engagement status indication:
► PTT Activation - Pressing Push To Talk button engages radio transmission
► Priority Management - Voice communication takes precedence during engagement

❕ Important: Engagement status protects ongoing communications from interruption by incoming DSC calls or other system functions.

► Manual Position Entry - Backup capability when GPS unavailable or inaccurate

EMERGENCY DISTRESS PROCEDURES
Emergency distress communication provides the ultimate safety net for vessels in grave and imminent danger, utilizing both automated DSC alerting and voice communication to coordinate rescue operations with maritime authorities and nearby vessels.

Distress procedures integrate sophisticated automated systems with straightforward manual operations, ensuring reliable emergency communication capability regardless of crew experience level or stress conditions during actual emergencies.

Distress Alert Transmission
Emergency distress alerting combines immediate automated DSC transmission with follow-up voice communication to provide comprehensive emergency notification and enable coordinated rescue response.

Basic distress transmission procedure:
1. Emergency Access - Lift protective cover over red distress button
2. Alert Activation - Press and hold distress 
3. System Response - CH70 and TX indicators appears
4. Acknowledgment Watch - Radio monitors Channel 70 for DSC acknowledgment
5. Automatic Repetition - System retransmits distress after 3.5-4.5 minute intervals
6. Voice Communication - Begin Channel 16 voice communication when acknowledged
7. Rescue Coordination - Provide detailed distress information to rescue services

Enhanced Distress Alert Procedures
Advanced distress alerting enables specification of emergency nature through pre-configured distress categories that provide rescue services with immediate situation awareness for appropriate response coordination.

Distress nature specification enhances rescue response effectiveness by providing specific emergency information that enables proper resource allocation and response planning before voice contact establishment.

Distress nature categories:
• Fire or Explosion - Shipboard fire emergencies requiring specialized response
• Flooding - Hull breach or water ingress threatening vessel safety
• Collision - Impact with other vessels, structures, or underwater obstacles
• Grounding - Vessel aground requiring assistance or rescue
• Listing - Dangerous heel indicating potential capsizing threat
• Sinking - Imminent vessel loss requiring immediate evacuation
• Disabled and Adrift - Loss of propulsion or steering capability
• Abandoning Ship - Crew evacuation in progress or planned
• Piracy Attack - Armed robbery or hijacking incident
• Man Overboard - Person in water requiring rescue assistance

Enhanced alert transmission:
► ALERT Function
► Nature Selection
► Position Verification
► Alert Confirmation
► Distress Activation
► System Integration

Good to Know
ATIS code programming provides automatic transmitter identification for European inland waterways, with foreign leisure craft codes typically based on MMSI numbers with 9 prefix.

Channel bandwidth selection between 25 kHz wide and 12.5 kHz narrow configurations affects channel numbering, with narrow channels showing 2xx or 4xx designations.

Weather channel availability varies by region, with WX prefixed channels available only in US and Canadian frequency allocations for weather broadcast reception.

Private channel programming supports up to 100 licensed frequencies for specialized operations, requiring dealer configuration for proper regulatory compliance.

Voice message storage automatically overwrites oldest recordings when capacity limit reached, with all messages deleted during power cycling.

DSC call logging maintains separate records for received distress calls, transmitted calls, and received non-distress communications with comprehensive session details.
