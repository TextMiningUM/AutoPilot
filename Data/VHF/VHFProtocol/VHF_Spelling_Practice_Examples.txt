SPELLING PRACTICE FOR VHF RADIO COMMUNICATION

Spelling out a word correctly on VHF radio is a core operator skill, required whenever a vessel name, a person's surname, a place name, or a technical term could be misheard due to static, weak signal, or similarly-sounding letters. Before spelling, the operator says "I SPELL" so the listening station knows individual letters follow rather than a normal word, then gives each letter using its NATO phonetic code word, and finally says "I SPELL" again followed by "OVER" to indicate the spelling is complete and hand the transmission back. The listening station should then read the spelled word back to confirm it was understood correctly. The examples below cover a wide range of realistic situations an operator will encounter: vessel names, surnames, given names, place names, waypoint and buoy names, cargo and technical terms, company and callsign fragments, harbor and port names, weather and position terms, and radio equipment terms.
