# VHF PROCEDURES

**For safe and efficient shipping traffic in the Scheldt area**

Version 7.4 — Oct 2025
Joint Nautical Authority (Gemeenschappelijk Nautisch Beheer) — Scheldt Area

---

## FOREWORD

These VHF procedures are a guideline for traffic controllers and traffic participants in the Scheldt area. Their aim is to clarify the use of VHF for safe and smooth shipping traffic. By correctly applying the procedures, other traffic participants will follow the good example, which will benefit safety.

The working procedures are based on the *IMO Guidelines For Vessel Traffic Services* (IMO Resolution A.1158(32)) and on the *IALA Guideline 1132 VTS Voice Communications and Phraseology*. In addition, various extra VHF regulations apply to specific vessel types, such as large gas tankers or oversized seagoing vessels. These regulations are not included in the general shipping procedures but are published via Joint Notices at www.vts-scheldt.net.

This document should be read together with the folder *VHF Sectors in the Scheldt Area*.

## 1. Definitions

**Duty to be reachable (Bereikbaarheidsplicht)**
Every vessel equipped with a VHF radio must always be reachable on the correct VHF channel, as stated in these procedures.

**Port information**
Information about bridges, berths and lock planning.

**Pilot advice under Pilotage from a Distance / Remote Pilotage (LOA)**
If a pilot cannot perform his or her duties on board the vessel being piloted, he or she may give advice remotely to a master and/or traffic participant. That advice may be given from another ship or from ashore (a traffic centre), under certain conditions.

**Overflow function**
The use of an additional VHF channel to relieve the regular traffic channel, for example to provide navigation assistance or to conduct other lengthy conversations.

**Radar channel**
This channel has an overflow function relative to the traffic channel. A radar channel is used to relieve the traffic channel, for example to provide navigation assistance or port information, or to conduct other conversations. Reachability on the traffic channel remains mandatory in the meantime.

**Traffic instruction (Verkeersaanwijzing)**
An order or prohibition imposed to bring about a specific result in traffic behaviour. The order or prohibition is imposed by an authorised person on one or more traffic participants.

**Traffic arrangement (Verkeersafspraak)**
An agreement made directly between traffic participants themselves, in order to prevent unclear situations and/or imminent danger. Traffic arrangements must be made directly between traffic participants and not via a traffic centre.

**Traffic centre**
The location from which a traffic controller carries out his or her duties. Within the GNB (Joint Nautical Management) there are five traffic centres.

**Traffic participant**
A participant who has actual control over the navigation of a vessel.

**General traffic information**
Information provided by a traffic controller relating to one or more traffic participants within the area of operation. The information may be intended to support the on-board navigational decision-making process, to monitor and plan vessels, or to respond to the development of unsafe situations.

**Traffic controller**
A certified person who, from a traffic centre, ensures the safe and smooth handling of shipping traffic.

**Mandatory reports**
Reports that traffic participants must make at prescribed points or times in order to keep shipping traffic safe and flowing smoothly.

**Area of operation**
The area of operation of the VHF procedures is indicated in the VHF Sectors document.

**Self-regulation**
Traffic participants may make traffic arrangements among themselves, without the intervention of a traffic centre. The traffic controller monitors the feasibility of the arrangements made.

## 2. Purpose of a Vessel Traffic Service

A Vessel Traffic Service is a service implemented by the authorities that provides the means to communicate with shipping traffic and to respond to situations developing within the VTS area. The service increases the safety and efficiency of shipping and contributes to "Safety of Life at Sea" and to environmental protection.

A VTS is responsible for:

- **Providing timely and relevant information** about factors that may influence navigation and that support the on-board decision-making process.
  This includes, among other things, information on the position, identity and destination of other ships, and hydrological and meteorological information.

- **Monitoring and planning vessels** so as to contribute to the safe and smooth handling of shipping traffic.
  This involves providing essential, up-to-date and timely information to support the on-board navigational decision-making process. This support consists of providing timely information, advice and instructions. The information may relate to anything in the chain of operations, such as lock planning, tidal windows, the availability of pilotage services, and so on.

- **Providing timely and relevant information** to respond to the development of unsafe situations.
  Essential, up-to-date and timely information is provided to support the on-board navigational decision-making process. This support consists of providing timely information, advice and traffic instructions.

To achieve this, a VTS can provide information, warnings or traffic instructions where necessary.

## 3. User philosophy

If users of VHF channels do not observe communication discipline, cross-talk occurs. This cross-talk, also known as interference, disrupts normal message traffic and creates questions that do not benefit safety. To prevent interference on the VHF channels, VHF users must observe the following rules:

- All conversations over VHF must be short, businesslike and clear, and take place on the correct channel (communication discipline).
- All VHF installations must transmit at a power of no more than 1 watt.
- Within VTS-SG, a reporting obligation and listening-watch obligation applies to all commercial shipping.
- For recreational craft with a VHF installation on board, a duty to be reachable applies, but not a reporting obligation (see the folder *VHF Sectors in the Scheldt Area*).
- Monitoring the traffic channel is mandatory at all times. In case of congestion or other circumstances, a radar channel can serve as an overflow.
- If a traffic controller foresees bottlenecks or dangerous situations, he or she actively intervenes to prevent problems. The traffic controller uses his or her authority to give a warning, information, advice or a traffic instruction.
- Without prejudice to the powers of the Flemish and Dutch authorities, and in the context of the safe and smooth handling of shipping traffic, ultimate responsibility for navigation always rests with the master/traffic participant.
- Traffic arrangements between ships, such as passing or overtaking not in accordance with the applicable navigation rules, are made directly between the ships themselves. When crossing, reference is usually made to the colour of the sidelights, for example: "We are passing green to green." When overtaking, reference is usually made to the side, for example: "I am passing on the port/starboard side." This is the traffic-regulating character of such arrangements.
- All traffic participants and traffic controllers must comply with these prescribed VHF procedures.

### 3.1 Communication discipline for VTS Scheldt Area participants

- **Maintain communication discipline**, even if other participants do not.
- **Use the ship's name and the traffic centre's own name** in a call, not abbreviations or private names.
- Traffic participants with a reporting obligation **report in on the relevant channel**. Reporting off again is not necessary, unless a mandatory report applies.
- Traffic controllers ensure that **traffic participants make traffic arrangements among themselves** via the traffic channels. They may also mediate in this.
- **Do not hold discussions** on traffic channels.
- Use only the **approved working languages: Dutch or English**. In English, preferably use the Standard Marine Communication Phrases (SMCP). In an emergency, deviation from this is permitted.
- Use **message markers** (in both Dutch and English) to make clear the type of message.

In the management area of the GNA (Joint Nautical Authority), the Permanent Committee on Supervision of Scheldt Shipping has determined that the national language is also the working language in VHF communication. That is Dutch, with English as a fallback option (in accordance with SMCP).

## 4. Working language

### 4.1 Language proficiency check

If there is doubt as to whether a waterway user is proficient in one of the mandatory working languages, the traffic controller will carry out a language proficiency check upon entry into the VTS-SG.

In the context of the chain of operations, the authorities of the ports adjoining the VTS-SG have issued similar instructions to their operational staff.

If the waterway user does not respond sufficiently clearly to a language proficiency check, the ship will not be admitted to the management area.

### 4.2 All shipping

The waterway user is addressed and guided in one of the working languages (Dutch and/or English). Deviation from this is only permitted to prevent an undesirable situation or an incident. The message must then be immediately repeated in Dutch and/or English, so that the other traffic participants know what was said.

### 4.3 Inland passenger vessels

If an inland passenger vessel is expected in the GNA's management area, the GNA checks in advance whether the master/skipper is proficient in one of the working languages.

## 5. Indicators

To promote communication discipline and to prevent overloading of the VHF channels in the VTS blocks, traffic controllers and all waterway users use the internationally established "message markers" to keep communication short, businesslike and clear.

These are the following eight indicators:

**MESSAGE MARKERS**

| Marker | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Information | Message contains observed facts |
| Question | Message has a questioning nature |
| Answer | Message is an answer to an earlier question |
| Intention | Message concerns intended navigational measures |
| Warning | Message is a warning of a potential danger |
| Request | Message asks the addressee(s) to take action |
| Advice | Sender wants to influence the addressee by means of a recommendation |
| Instruction | Sender wants to influence the addressee by means of a traffic instruction |

## 6. Navigation assistance

The waterway user indicates when navigation assistance should be started and stopped by reporting in and out on the radar channel. The term **"navigation assistance"** is always used for this.¹

A traffic controller may also initiate the use of (elements of) navigation assistance on their own initiative in the event of deviant sailing behaviour.

- The traffic controller will confirm that the ship wants navigation assistance.
- In addition, the traffic controller will ask seagoing vessels in particular whether "buoy transits" (doorwisselen van boeien) are also desired.
  - Answer no: the traffic controller does not include the reporting of buoy transits as standard in the report.
  - Answer yes: the traffic controller includes the report of buoy transits (in a bend or during major course changes) as standard in the report.
- The waterway user then receives an initial message containing the following elements:
  - Understood/Acknowledged.
  - The own position of the ship that reported in for navigation assistance, according to the along/across method or the Bearing and Distance method (B/D method).
  - A relevant traffic picture of the ships in the vicinity.
- The traffic controller will also provide updates with the following content:
  - Own position of the ship according to the along/across or B/D method.
  - The aim is to provide this at least at every buoy or every second buoy, according to the traffic controller's assessment.
  - Update of the relevant² traffic picture, whereby work must also be done across block boundaries. Extra attention is given in this traffic picture to relevant overtaking manoeuvres and crossing traffic.
  - Any relevant passing distances, where applicable.
- Traffic arrangements must always be made on the traffic channel.

¹ No response is given to a request for assistance if reporting in is done using the terms "standing by" or "monitoring" on the radar channel.
² The information relevant to the traffic participant under navigation assistance (traffic, particulars, etc.) needed to form an up-to-date traffic picture in order to navigate smoothly, safely and efficiently.

### Explanation

- Traffic participants must indicate in good time if they wish to deviate from a route.
- During navigation assistance, the traffic controller gives an overview of the current traffic picture, possibly supplemented with calculated, expected situations, such as encounters and passages in (passing) distance and time.
- To convey the position of a moving ship, the position of the bow of the vessel relative to a point ahead in the fairway, or in bearing and distance, is used. For a stationary ship, this is the middle of the track or a radar echo.
- The **passing distance** is the distance that will exist during passing between the adjacent sides of two ships, or between a ship and an obstacle. The passing distance is only reliable provided the ground track remains constant.
- The **ground track** of a ship is its direction of movement relative to true north over the ground.
- The **distance between two vessels** is the measured shortest distance. For head-on traffic this is the bow-to-bow distance; for overtaking traffic this is the bow-to-stern distance.
- The **abeam distance** to a buoy, beacon or obstacle is the distance between the bow of a moving ship and an object, measured perpendicular to the direction of the fairway.
- The **distance to a navigation mark** is the shortest distance to that navigation mark.
- The terms **"opvarend" (upbound)** and **"afvarend" (downbound)** are used east of the Schone Waardin. In English these are "ingoing" and "outgoing".
- In the approach areas and the roadstead area, the terms **"inkomend" (inbound)** and **"uitgaand" (outbound)** are used, in English "inbound" and "outbound".
- To indicate a position before or beyond a certain point, we use **"bovenwaarts" (upstream)** or **"benedenwaarts" (downstream)**. In English these are "above" and "below".
- If the traffic controller must give information about a part of the area not covered by radar and for which no visual information is available, he or she will make this known to the traffic participant requesting the information.
- Position information is always given relative to known reference points. These points are visible, known and can be found on the nautical chart.
- A **bearing** between two known points is the horizontal angle relative to true north and the point being observed. The figures are spoken one by one.
- **Distances** are given to a tenth of a kilometre or a tenth of a nautical mile (also called a "cable"). Where confusion is possible, e.g. between the English numbers fifty and fifteen, the digits are spoken separately.
- Names of buoys and markers must not be translated. They are pronounced as they appear on the nautical chart. For example, W6 is pronounced "Whiskey 6".
- "Buoy transit" (doorwisselen van boeien) refers to two consecutive buoys marking a bend in a channel coming into line relative to the bow of the ship.
- Position reports can be given in two ways: using the along/across method, or via bearing and distance. The traffic controller may supplement this with ground track and ground speed. The intervals between reports depend on the traffic picture, the speeds of vessels, meteorological conditions, nautically critical points, and so on.
- An **along/across position report** comprises, on the one hand, the point to which the ship has progressed along the length of the fairway, and on the other hand, the distance of the ship in the transverse direction, measured to the locally customary reference line — for example the buoy line, the light line, the shore, etc. The transverse distance may also be expressed as 1/3 red, 1/3 green, or mid-channel. If the distance is less than 1/3 of the fairway width, the distance is expressed in metres to the buoy line.
  Measurements are always given relative to the starboard side (SB) of the vessel. If this is not possible because there is no reference, the traffic controller will explicitly state this.
- A **position report via bearing and distance** looks at the position of the forward part of the assisted ship in relation to a known point.
  Here too, the traffic controller may add ground track and ground speed. If the ship is sailing parallel to the reference line, moving away from it, or approaching it, this will be stated.
- If the traffic controller gives position reports (on request) while a ship is coming to anchor, he or she agrees which point the reports will be relative to. Either the planned anchor position is indicated — this may be an anchorage as shown on the chart, or a position chosen by the master or pilot. From the bow, the traffic controller gives information by bearing and distance (B/D) to the anchor position, including speed over ground. As a standard for reporting frequency, the following applies:
  - Distance more than 1500 m: B/D every 500 m
  - Distance 1500 to 500 m: B/D every 200 m
  - Distance 500 to 200 m: B/D every 100 m
  - From 200 m: B/D every 50 m.

  For anchor watch purposes, the master or pilot must state the number of nautical miles and/or cables relative to a fixed point, and the number of shackles on deck.

## 7. VHF channels

VHF channels are classified as follows according to their use:

### 7.1 Traffic channels
- Traffic arrangements
- Traffic information
- Pilot advice
- Traffic instructions
- Mandatory reports
- Port information (if no port information channel is available)

### 7.2 Radar channels
- Traffic information
- Navigation assistance
- Mandatory reports
- Port information (if no port information channel is available)

### 7.3 Emergency channel (VHF 67)
An emergency channel is a VHF channel intended exclusively for handling radio traffic during emergencies. The competent authority refers VHF users to the emergency channel where there is reason to do so.

### 7.4 Port information channel (Port Operations)
Information on berths, locks, waiting quays, anchorages, service providers, etc.

### 7.5 Other channels
- Pilotage service channels
- Port channel
- Lock/bridge channels
- Working channels

## 10. Scheldt Shipping Reports (SSB)

### 10.1 Principles

The Scheldt Shipping Report (SSB) aims to provide information of a general nature to waterway users. The content of the SSB includes:

- Water levels and expected deviations at various measuring points in the area
- Expected cross-current reports off buoy 53
- Wind direction and strength at the traffic centre, storm warnings, local wind forecasts
- Visibility reports (where applicable)
- Shipping traffic, particulars and important works
- Significant deviations in fairway marking
- Depending on the traffic centre: pilotage information such as the side of the pilot ladder, storm pilotage, significant wave height at Kwintebank (GHs KWI) from 2.0 metres, and so on
- For Zeebrugge Traffic Centre only: traffic pictures within the area of operation concerning ships with a draught of ≥140 dm, or ships which, due to their tidal window (GNA), cannot deviate

### 10.2 Area description, VHF channels and times

Four area-specific Scheldt Shipping Reports are broadcast. These are broadcast at different times so that they do not overlap.

#### 10.2.1 Zeebrugge Traffic Centre
Wandelaar area, Zeebrugge area, and Vlissingen area up to the eastern boundary of the Vlissingen Roadstead precautionary area (= meridian over the Sloehaven green light).

- Channel 69 in Dutch, at the hour + 10'
- Channel 04 in English, at the hour + 15'
- Channel 69 in English, at the hour + 40' (information for oversized seagoing vessels only)
- Channels 60 and 04 in English, at the hour + 15'

#### 10.2.2 Vlissingen Traffic Centre
Steenbank area, Zeebrugge area, Vlissingen area, Terneuzen area and Hansweert area.

- Channel 14 in Dutch, at the hour + 50'
- Channel 21 in English, at the hour + 55'

#### 10.2.3 Zandvliet Traffic Centre
Antwerpen area, Hansweert area, Terneuzen area and Vlissingen area up to the eastern boundary of the Vlissingen Roadstead precautionary area (= meridian over the Sloehaven green light).

- Channel 12 in Dutch, at the hour + 30'

#### 10.2.4 Terneuzen Traffic Centre
Ghent–Terneuzen Canal area and Terneuzen lock complex.

- Channel 11 in Dutch, at the hour + 0'
