ROUTINE, PROCEDURE-WORD AND URGENCY CALL DEMONSTRATIONS

The examples below show exactly what to transmit, word for word, for common routine communications, correct procedure-word usage, and urgency (PAN PAN) calls. Each example first explains the situation, then gives the precise transmission an operator should use. Following the exact wording matters: VHF radio discipline depends on short, standardized phrases so that other stations and coast guards immediately understand what is happening, without wasted airtime or ambiguity.

Opening A Routine Call

To open a routine call to another vessel, state the called station's name twice, then your own station's name twice, then invite a reply. Example: "SEA OTTER, SEA OTTER, THIS IS NORTHERN STAR, NORTHERN STAR, OVER." The double repetition of each name reduces the chance of a mis-heard call sign on a noisy channel.

To open a routine call to a harbour or port control, use the same double-repeat structure and immediately say what you want. Example: "PORT CONTROL, PORT CONTROL, THIS IS MV AMBER TIDE, MV AMBER TIDE, REQUESTING PERMISSION TO ENTER, OVER."

Minimal-Words Routine Calls

A concise routine call includes only identity, timing or position, and the single action requested, then closes cleanly. Example: "LOCK CONTROL, THIS IS BLUE FALCON, TWO MINUTES OUT, LOCK ONE, PERMISSION TO ENTER, OVER." On reply: "BLUE FALCON, THIS IS LOCK CONTROL, ROGER, LOCK ONE, OUT." Nothing beyond identity, timing, and the one request is included; this is the standard for routine calls under the SMCP.

Another concise example, requesting a berth: "HARBOUR MASTER, THIS IS SILVER GULL, REQUEST BERTH ASSIGNMENT, OVER." Reply: "SILVER GULL, THIS IS HARBOUR MASTER, BERTH FOUR, OVER." Close: "HARBOUR MASTER, THIS IS SILVER GULL, ROGER, BERTH FOUR, OUT."

Logging Routine Communication

When a routine communication carries operationally important information, such as pilotage advice, it must be logged with UTC time, the channel used, the other station's name, a factual summary of the advice given, any instruction to change channel, and confirmation that the message was read back. Example log entry: "1430 UTC, Channel 12, SOLENT VTS advised recommended course 270, holding position off Fairway Buoy, tidal window closes 1600, instructed to monitor Channel 12, advice read back and confirmed."

A second logging example, for a traffic report: "0915 UTC, Channel 71, DOVER COASTGUARD reported inbound traffic in the Dover Strait, TSS crossing recommended at 090 degrees, instructed to report position again at the Varne buoy, message read back and confirmed."

Closing A Routine Call Correctly

After the substantive exchange is complete, close by stating which channel you will now monitor, then say OUT. Example: "STORM PETREL, THIS IS CRIMSON WAVE, STANDING BY ON CHANNEL SIXTEEN, OUT." If returning to the international calling and distress channel: "STORM PETREL, THIS IS CRIMSON WAVE, RETURNING TO CHANNEL ONE-SIX, OUT."

The pro-words OVER and OUT are never used together. OVER means a reply is expected; OUT means the exchange is finished and no reply is expected. Saying "OVER AND OUT" is a common error and is never correct procedure, because it contradicts itself: it invites a reply while also announcing that none is expected.

Requesting A Channel Change During A Routine Call

To move a routine conversation off the calling channel, state the proposed working channel and wait for acknowledgement before switching. Example: "GOLDEN TERN, THIS IS MISTY HARBOR, SWITCH CHANNEL SEVENTY-THREE, OVER." Reply: "MISTY HARBOR, THIS IS GOLDEN TERN, CHANNEL SEVENTY-THREE, OVER." Both stations then move to Channel 73 before continuing.

Routine Position And Traffic Reporting To A VTS

When reporting to a Vessel Traffic Service on request, give vessel name, position, course, speed, and destination in that order, then invite further instruction. Example: "THAMES VTS, THIS IS WINDWARD ISLE, POSITION FIFE WUN DECIMAL FOWER ZERO NORTH ZERO ZERO DECIMAL TOO AIT EAST, COURSE ZERO NINER ZERO, SPEED TWELVE KNOTS, BOUND FOR TILBURY, OVER." Reply: "WINDWARD ISLE, THIS IS THAMES VTS, ROGER, REPORT AGAIN AT NORTHFLEET, OUT."

Procedure Word: I SPELL

The pro-word I SPELL introduces a letter-by-letter phonetic spelling of a word already spoken in plain language, and the plain word is repeated once more afterward so the listener can confirm it. Example: "THIS IS COPPER KESTREL, DESTINATION SCHEVENINGEN, I SPELL SIERRA CHARLIE HOTEL ECHO VICTOR ECHO NOVEMBER INDIA NOVEMBER GOLF ECHO NOVEMBER, SCHEVENINGEN, OVER."

A second example, spelling a vessel name during a routine identification: "THIS IS OCEAN FALCON, I SPELL OSCAR CHARLIE ECHO ALPHA NOVEMBER, FOXTROT ALPHA LIMA CHARLIE OSCAR NOVEMBER, OCEAN FALCON, OVER."

Procedure Word: CORRECTION

The pro-word CORRECTION explicitly voids whatever was just said and signals that the corrected version follows immediately, within the same transmission. Example: "THIS IS RAVENSBROOK, MY POSITION IS FIFE ZERO WUN TOO DECIMAL FIFE NORTH, ZERO ZERO WUN FOWER FIFE DECIMAL TREE WEST, CORRECTION, ZERO ZERO WUN FOWER FIFE DECIMAL TREE EAST, OVER."

A second CORRECTION example: "THIS IS DUNCAIRN, REQUESTING BERTH SIX, CORRECTION, BERTH SIXTEEN, OVER." The corrected figure always comes immediately after the word CORRECTION, never as a separate follow-up transmission.

Procedure Word: SAY AGAIN

SAY AGAIN is used to request a repeat of all or part of a message, and should specify exactly what is unclear whenever possible. Example: "THIS IS HARTSHAVEN, YOUR SIGNAL IS UNREADABLE, SAY AGAIN YOUR ENTIRE MESSAGE, OVER." A more specific version: "THIS IS EASTMERE, YOUR SIGNAL IS BROKEN, SAY AGAIN ALL AFTER POSITION, OVER."

When responding to a SAY AGAIN request, repeat the requested information slowly and without paraphrasing, digit by digit if it is numerical. Example: "THIS IS FENHOLM, I SAY AGAIN, MY POSITION IS FIFE ZERO DECIMAL WUN TOO MINUTES NORTH, PERSONS ON BOARD FIFE, I SAY AGAIN, PERSONS ON BOARD FIFE, OVER."

Procedure Words: ROGER And WILCO

ROGER means the previous transmission was received and understood; it does not mean agreement or compliance. WILCO means "I have received your message, understood it, and will comply." Example exchange: "THIS IS CRAGMOUTH, PROCEED TO ANCHORAGE BRAVO, OVER." Reply: "THIS IS ASHWORTH BAY, WILCO, OVER." A separate example where only acknowledgement, not compliance, is intended: "THIS IS GREYWATER POINT, ROGER, OVER."

Procedure Words: AFFIRMATIVE And NEGATIVE

AFFIRMATIVE means yes; NEGATIVE means no. Both are used instead of a plain "yes" or "no" because they are less likely to be misheard over a noisy channel. Example: "THIS IS MAINSTAY, CONFIRM YOU ARE MAKING WAY, OVER." Reply: "THIS IS OUTER LEDGE, AFFIRMATIVE, MAKING WAY AT SIX KNOTS, OVER." A NEGATIVE example: "THIS IS NORTH RACE, CONFIRM DECK CARGO SECURE, OVER." Reply: "THIS IS WHISTLING ROCK, NEGATIVE, SECURING NOW, OVER."

The Distinction Between OVER And OUT

OVER hands the transmission to the other station and invites a reply. OUT ends the exchange and states that no reply is expected. A station never says both together. Correct mid-conversation use: "THIS IS MID CHANNEL, REQUEST YOUR INTENTIONS, OVER." Correct conversation-ending use: "THIS IS FAIRWAY GATE, INTENTIONS UNDERSTOOD, OUT."

Full PAN PAN Urgency Call Structure

A formal urgency call begins with the urgency signal spoken three times, followed by ALL STATIONS spoken three times, then the vessel's identity spoken three times, then MMSI, position, the nature of the urgency, and a request for assistance. Example: "PAN PAN, PAN PAN, PAN PAN, ALL STATIONS, ALL STATIONS, ALL STATIONS, THIS IS SKYWARD NINER, SKYWARD NINER, SKYWARD NINER, MMSI TOO TREE FIFE ZERO ZERO WUN TOO TREE FOWER, POSITION FIFE ZERO DECIMAL TOO NORTH, ZERO ZERO WUN DECIMAL FIFE WEST, LOSS OF STEERING GEAR, DRIFTING WITH WIND TOWARDS THE COAST AT FOWER KNOTS, AMPLE SEA ROOM AT PRESENT, FOWER PERSONS ON BOARD, REQUESTING ASSISTANCE OR A TOW, OVER."

A second full urgency example for an engine failure adrift: "PAN PAN, PAN PAN, PAN PAN, ALL STATIONS, ALL STATIONS, ALL STATIONS, THIS IS TRIDENT LINE, TRIDENT LINE, TRIDENT LINE, MMSI TOO TREE FIFE WUN WUN TOO TOO WUN WUN, POSITION FOWER NINER DECIMAL AIT NORTH, ZERO ZERO TOO DECIMAL WUN EAST, TOTAL ENGINE FAILURE, DRIFTING, NO IMMEDIATE DANGER, TOO PERSONS ON BOARD, REQUESTING TOW ASSISTANCE, OVER."

A third full urgency example for a person requiring medical attention that is not immediately life-threatening: "PAN PAN, PAN PAN, PAN PAN, ALL STATIONS, ALL STATIONS, ALL STATIONS, THIS IS COASTAL GUARDIAN, COASTAL GUARDIAN, COASTAL GUARDIAN, MMSI TOO TREE FOWER NINER NINER NINER ZERO ZERO WUN, POSITION FIFE WUN DECIMAL WUN NORTH, ZERO ZERO WUN DECIMAL TREE EAST, CREW MEMBER WITH SUSPECTED BROKEN LIMB, CONSCIOUS AND STABLE, TREE PERSONS ON BOARD, REQUESTING MEDICAL ADVICE, OVER."

Checking Whether A Situation Warrants An Urgency Call

If unsure whether a situation is serious enough for a formal PAN PAN, call the coastguard first on their working channel and ask for advice rather than escalating immediately. Example: "SOLENT COASTGUARD, THIS IS PELAGIC, ROUTINE, REQUEST ADVICE, OVER." After describing the situation factually, the coastguard will advise whether to declare a formal urgency call, continue monitoring, or stand down. This avoids both over-reacting and under-reacting to a developing situation.

Responding To A Request To Repeat Urgency Information

When a coast station asks to repeat critical information, respond promptly, slowly, and without paraphrasing, spelling out numbers digit by digit. Example: "THIS IS NORSECREST, I SAY AGAIN, MY POSITION IS FIFE ZERO DECIMAL WUN TOO MINUTES NORTH, PERSONS ON BOARD FIFE, I SAY AGAIN, PERSONS ON BOARD FIFE, OVER." The voluntary use of I SAY AGAIN for safety-critical facts, even before being asked, is good practice.

Urgency Call For Taking On Water, Not Yet Sinking

Example: "PAN PAN, PAN PAN, PAN PAN, ALL STATIONS, ALL STATIONS, ALL STATIONS, THIS IS BALTIFREIGHT, BALTIFREIGHT, BALTIFREIGHT, MMSI TOO TREE FIFE TOO TOO TOO ZERO ZERO WUN, POSITION FIFE ZERO DECIMAL FIFE NORTH, ZERO ZERO WUN DECIMAL AIT WEST, TAKING ON WATER, PUMPS CONTAINING IT FOR NOW, SIX PERSONS ON BOARD, REQUESTING STANDBY ASSISTANCE, OVER."

Urgency Call After A Person Overboard Has Been Recovered Safely

Once a person overboard has been recovered unharmed, a PAN PAN cancellation or "all clear" broadcast should follow so other stations do not continue diverting resources. Example: "ALL STATIONS, ALL STATIONS, ALL STATIONS, THIS IS OCEANEX, OCEANEX, OCEANEX, PAN PAN CANCEL, PERSON OVERBOARD RECOVERED SAFELY, NO FURTHER ASSISTANCE REQUIRED, OUT."
